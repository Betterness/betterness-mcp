# Evidence Bundle: Attia Longevity Framework

## Thesis

Longevity is operationalized through measurable risk reduction across the four major domains of morbidity: cardiovascular disease, metabolic dysfunction, neurodegenerative disease, and cancer. Lifespan and healthspan are improved not by chasing marginal optimization, but by systematically identifying and reducing the highest-probability threats to function and survival. The earlier the intervention, the greater the compounding benefit.

## Primary Authors and Frameworks

- Peter Attia, MD — Outlive: The Science and Art of Longevity (2023). Systematic framework for longevity medicine, risk stratification, and the "four horsemen" model of chronic disease.
- Preventive cardiology literature — cardiovascular risk reduction, ApoB, lipid management, blood pressure targets.
- Metabolic medicine — insulin resistance as an upstream driver of multiple chronic conditions.
- Exercise physiology — VO2 max and muscular strength as survival predictors.

## Key Lenses

**ApoB** — The primary cardiovascular risk biomarker. More predictive than LDL-C because it counts all atherogenic particles. Target <60 mg/dL for high-risk individuals, <80 mg/dL as a general population goal. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in most developed countries; ApoB is the most actionable upstream lever.

**Insulin sensitivity** — Measured via fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, or oral glucose tolerance testing. Insulin resistance precedes and contributes to type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and some cancers. Reversible in early stages through exercise, dietary composition, and sleep.

**VO2 max** — Maximum oxygen uptake is the single strongest predictor of all-cause mortality available in clinical practice. Moving from the bottom to the top quartile of VO2 max for age is associated with a 5x reduction in mortality risk across multiple large observational studies. This is a trainable variable at any age.

**Muscle mass and strength** — Low muscle mass (sarcopenia) and grip strength are independently associated with mortality, functional decline, and metabolic disease. Resistance training builds the reserve needed to maintain function through aging-related decline. Start building in the 30s and 40s, not the 70s.

**Sleep** — Chronic insufficient sleep (< 7 hours) accelerates metabolic dysfunction, cardiovascular risk, neurodegeneration, and immune impairment. Sleep is not optional. It is a tier-1 longevity lever with no pharmaceutical substitute.

**Emotional health** — Chronic unmanaged psychological stress, depression, and social isolation are associated with mortality risk comparable to smoking in observational data. The relationship between mental health and physical longevity is bidirectional and mechanistic.

## Evidence Standards

This bundle favors:
- Large prospective cohort studies (n >10,000 with long follow-up)
- RCTs with clinical endpoints (not just biomarker surrogates)
- Mechanistic studies with convergent human data
- Meta-analyses that pool high-quality individual studies

This bundle treats with caution:
- Single small RCTs without replication
- Cross-sectional studies for causal claims
- Supplement studies with industry funding and surrogate endpoints
- Longevity claims from short-duration interventions without biomarker validation

## Known Limits

**Individual variation is large.** Genetic factors (APOE status, familial hypercholesterolemia, BRCA variants) can substantially change individual risk profiles. Population-level evidence must be filtered through individual context.

**Observational limits.** Much longevity research is observational by necessity — randomizing people to 20-year lifestyle exposures is not feasible. Confounding in observational studies is a real limitation. Be explicit when a claim is "associated with" vs. "causally demonstrated."

**Evolving science.** Longevity medicine is a young field. Optimal protein targets, fasting protocols, hormone optimization windows, and epigenetic clock interventions are all actively debated. Hold positions proportionally to evidence strength.

**Access and feasibility.** Not all interventions are accessible to all populations. Expensive testing, specialized labs, and boutique interventions skew the longevity literature toward affluent research participants.
