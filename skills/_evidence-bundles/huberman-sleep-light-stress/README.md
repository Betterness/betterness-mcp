# Evidence Bundle: Sleep, Light, and Stress — Neuroscience Foundations

## Thesis

Sleep, light exposure, and stress regulation are the foundational biological regulators of health performance. They are upstream of nutrition, exercise, and supplementation — disrupt them, and every other health intervention underperforms. Optimize them through evidence-based behavioral protocols, and the system self-corrects across multiple domains simultaneously. The central insight is mechanistic: understanding the biology of adenosine, circadian timing, cortisol, and melatonin reveals why specific protocols work and enables precise behavioral prescriptions rather than generic sleep hygiene advice.

## Key Lenses

**Adenosine** — Sleep pressure molecule. Builds continuously during waking hours, cleared during non-REM sleep. Caffeine works by blocking adenosine receptors — borrowing wakefulness, not creating it. The adenosine debt is paid later as a deeper sleep drive. Delaying caffeine 90 minutes post-wake allows the natural adenosine + cortisol morning alertness system to function before caffeine intervenes.

**Cortisol** — The morning cortisol pulse (cortisol awakening response) is the primary circadian anchor. Morning light exposure to the low-angle sun activates a neural pathway that amplifies this pulse, sharpening circadian timing. Evening cortisol elevation (from stress, bright light, or stimulants) delays melatonin onset and fragments sleep architecture.

**Melatonin** — Melatonin onset signals darkness, not sleepiness directly. It is the circadian darkness marker. It rises ~2 hours before habitual sleep time in a normal, light-entrained circadian system. Blue-rich artificial light at night suppresses melatonin by up to 50% for 3+ hours post-exposure. Melatonin supplementation at physiological doses (0.1–0.5 mg) can advance circadian phase; high doses (5–10 mg) are pharmacological and exceed natural plasma levels significantly.

**Circadian timing** — The suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus is the master clock, entrained primarily by light. Every cell in the body has its own peripheral clock synchronized downstream. Circadian misalignment — when behavioral timing (sleep, meals, light) conflicts with biological clock — impairs metabolic function, hormonal regulation, immune activity, and cognitive performance.

**Temperature** — Core body temperature drops 1–3°F to initiate sleep and rises to wakefulness. The sleep environment temperature, pre-sleep behavioral choices (hot bath timing, evening exercise), and circadian phase all modulate this. The correct use of a hot bath before bed: it dilates peripheral blood vessels and dumps heat from the core, producing a net cooling effect that facilitates sleep onset — but must be taken 1–2 hours before bed, not immediately before.

**NSDR and physiological down-regulation** — Non-sleep deep rest (NSDR), yoga nidra, and specific breathwork (cyclic sighing, physiological sigh) activate parasympathetic tone and reduce cortisol reliably. These are evidence-based tools for daytime stress recovery and evening wind-down, not wellness marketing.

## Evidence Standards

This bundle favors:
- Peer-reviewed neuroscience and sleep science literature
- Mechanistic research with convergent human and animal evidence
- RCTs and controlled laboratory studies on behavioral interventions (light, caffeine timing, temperature)
- Wearable-based large dataset studies on sleep architecture

This bundle treats with caution:
- Single-supplement sleep studies with small samples
- Self-report sleep quality measures without objective validation
- Industry-funded supplement trials
- Circadian claims extrapolated from animal models without human evidence

## Known Limits

**Individual chronotype variation.** Circadian phase is partly genetically determined. True evening chronotypes (delayed sleep phase) cannot simply shift to early morning wakefulness through behavioral tools alone without significant difficulty. Acknowledge chronotype and recommend adaptation within realistic range.

**Mental health conditions substantially alter sleep neuroscience.** Depression affects REM architecture, anxiety disrupts sleep onset, PTSD disrupts sleep continuity. Standard protocols require clinical context. This evidence bundle does not cover clinical sleep disorder management.

**Sleep measurement limitations.** Consumer wearables measure sleep stages through movement and heart rate estimation, not polysomnography. They provide directionally useful data with meaningful accuracy limitations on sleep stage classification (particularly slow-wave vs. REM differentiation). Frame wearable sleep data as a trend tool, not clinical-grade architecture measurement.

**Supplement evidence gaps.** The behavioral intervention evidence (light, timing, temperature) is robust. The supplement evidence (magnesium, apigenin, glycine) is promising but often based on smaller studies. Be clear about the evidence differential.
