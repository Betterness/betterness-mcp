# Marathon — Planning Mode

## When Planning Mode Activates

Planning mode engages when the runner asks a question that requires reasoning across multiple data sources, time horizons, or training trade-offs — not just a daily verdict.

### Trigger questions

- "Should I adjust my training plan?"
- "I missed two weeks — how do I come back?"
- "Am I ready for this race?"
- "How should I taper?"
- "My pace has plateaued — what's going on?"
- "Plan my race week"
- "I have a niggle in my calf — should I run through it?"
- "What went wrong in my last marathon?"

---

## Planning Mode Protocol

### Step 1: Gather context

Before reasoning, collect:
1. Current training phase, week number, and race date (from local workspace memory)
2. Recent training data — volume, intensity, key session execution (via Betterness MCP)
3. Recovery trend — 7-day and 30-day HRV, sleep, readiness (via Betterness Graph)
4. Training load progression — acute vs chronic workload, fatigue accumulation (via Betterness Graph)
5. Known patterns — how this runner responds to volume changes, tapers, speed work (via Betterness Graph)
6. Lab biomarkers if available and relevant

### Step 2: Identify the real question

The runner may ask "am I ready for this race?" but the real question is usually one of:
- Have I done enough training to finish?
- Have I done enough training to hit my goal time?
- Is my body healthy enough to start?
- Should I adjust my goal based on what my training says?

Identify the actionable core before reasoning.

### Step 3: Reason with Betterness Graph first

Do not re-derive what Betterness Graph already knows.

Ask the graph:
- What is this runner's training load trend over the last 8 weeks?
- What are their personal recovery patterns after high-volume weeks?
- How have they responded to tapers in previous cycles?
- What correlations exist between their training data and race performance?

Use the graph's intelligence to shorten the reasoning chain.

### Step 4: Propose a plan

Structure the response as:

```
Where you are:
[Phase, week, volume trend, recovery status — 2 sentences max]

What the data says:
[Most relevant findings, ranked by importance]

What to do:
[Specific, actionable steps — not vague guidance]

What to watch:
[How to tell if it is working, and when to reassess]
```

### Step 5: Offer to go deeper

After delivering the plan, offer one or two optional deep dives:
- "Want me to pull your ferritin and cortisol labs into this?"
- "I can model your race pace based on your long run splits — want to see that?"
- "Should I adjust the taper timeline based on your recovery trend?"

## Common Planning Scenarios

### "Should I adjust my plan?"

1. Check actual vs planned volume and intensity for the past 3 weeks
2. Check recovery trend — is the runner absorbing the training load?
3. Identify the gap — are they ahead, behind, or drifting from the plan structure?
4. Propose specific adjustments tied to the remaining weeks before race day

### "I missed time — how do I come back?"

1. Establish how much time was missed and why (injury, illness, life)
2. Check current fitness markers via Betterness Graph (VO2max trend, training load decay)
3. Map remaining weeks to race day
4. Build a modified plan that prioritizes the most important sessions — do not try to cram missed volume
5. Adjust race goal if the gap is significant, honestly and with data

### "Am I ready for this race?"

1. Pull 12-week training load progression from Betterness Graph
2. Check long run completion — did the runner hit their longest runs at target pace?
3. Assess recovery trajectory — is the runner entering taper healthy or fatigued?
4. Compare to historical data if available (previous race performances, similar training blocks)
5. Deliver an honest readiness assessment with a recommended goal pace range

### "Plan my taper"

1. Confirm race date, current volume, current phase
2. Determine taper length based on runner's history (typically 2-3 weeks for marathon)
3. Map volume reduction — Week 1 of taper drops 20-25%, Week 2 drops 40-50%, race week is minimal
4. Maintain 1-2 short intensity touches (not volume) to keep legs sharp
5. Set fueling protocol — carb loading timing, race nutrition plan
6. Set heartbeat adjustments for the taper period (taper-specific messaging)

## Planning Mode Rules

1. **Always start with Betterness Graph context.** Do not reason from scratch when the graph has the training load trend.
2. **Rank factors by importance.** Do not present 8 possibilities. Lead with the one that matters most.
3. **Make plans specific.** "Run more" is not a plan. "Add 2 miles to your Tuesday easy run for the next 3 weeks" is a plan.
4. **Include a checkpoint.** Every plan should say when to reassess — usually 1-2 weeks.
5. **Do not over-plan.** One to three changes at a time. The runner has enough to think about.
6. **Be honest about goals.** If the data says the runner is not on track for their goal time, say so directly and propose a realistic alternative. False hope at mile 20 is cruel.
