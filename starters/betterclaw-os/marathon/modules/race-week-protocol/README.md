# Module: Race Week Protocol

Delivers a 7-day countdown to race day with daily guidance on training, fueling, logistics, sleep, and mental preparation.

## When Active

Shown during the 7 days before the target race date. Activates automatically when the calendar reaches race week. The most structured module in the Marathon — every day has specific actions.

## Data Sources

- Betterness MCP: `getWearableData` (recovery, sleep, HRV, resting HR)
- Betterness Graph: race history, taper response, carb loading effectiveness, race-day weather correlation
- Local memory: race details (name, date, start time, course profile, goal pace, logistics), fueling preferences, gear list

## The 7-Day Protocol

### Day 7 (Sunday before race)

```
Race Week — Day 7

Easy 4-5 miles. Last "real" run of the cycle.
Review your race plan: goal pace, fueling schedule, gear.
Sleep is a priority all week — aim for 8+ hours tonight.
Begin increasing carb intake slightly (not loading yet).
```

### Day 6 (Monday)

```
Race Week — Day 6

Rest day or easy 20-min shakeout.
Confirm race logistics: bib pickup hours, start corral,
parking or transit plan, bag check.
Eat normally but prioritize carbs at each meal.
```

### Day 5 (Tuesday)

```
Race Week — Day 5

Easy 3 miles with 4x100m strides at the end.
Keep the legs awake, nothing more.
Continue carb-focused eating. Hydrate well.
Check weather forecast — adjust race-day clothing plan.
```

### Day 4 (Wednesday)

```
Race Week — Day 4

Easy 3 miles at whatever pace feels good.
Lay out race kit tonight: shoes, bib, gels, body glide,
hat/sunglasses, watch (charged), throwaway layer.
Carb loading begins in earnest tomorrow.
```

### Day 3 (Thursday)

```
Race Week — Day 3

Easy 2 miles or rest. Your choice.
Carb loading day: 3-4g carbs per lb of body weight.
Rice, pasta, bread, potatoes, pretzels — simple carbs.
Nothing new. Nothing spicy. Nothing adventurous.

Pick up bib today if possible to avoid race-morning stress.
```

### Day 2 (Friday)

```
Race Week — Day 2

Rest. No running.
Continue carb loading. Same foods as yesterday.
Prep your race morning: alarm time, breakfast plan,
travel time to start. Add 30 min buffer for everything.

Lay out everything tonight. Pins on bib. Gels in belt.
Watch charged. Done.

Sleep will probably be restless tonight. That is normal.
Saturday night's sleep matters more anyway.
```

### Day 1 (Saturday — race eve)

```
Race Week — Day 1

Optional 10-15 min shakeout jog. Or rest. Either is fine.
Eat your pre-race dinner by 6 PM. Something familiar,
carb-heavy, easy to digest. Nothing new.

Review your pacing plan:
  Miles 1-6: goal pace + 5-10 sec (patient start)
  Miles 7-18: goal pace (settle in)
  Miles 19-22: hold pace (this is the race)
  Miles 23-26.2: whatever you have left

Sleep by 9 PM. Even if you do not sleep well, rest.
Set two alarms. Tomorrow you run.
```

### Race Day (Sunday)

```
Race Day

Wake: [calculated from start time — 3 hours before gun]
Breakfast: [pre-set meal] — eat 2.5-3 hours before start.
Arrive: 60 min before gun time.

Pacing:
  Miles 1-6: [goal + 5-10 sec] — bank nothing, stay patient
  Miles 7-18: [goal pace] — rhythm, fuel on schedule
  Miles 19-22: [goal pace] — this is where the race starts
  Miles 23-26.2: everything you have

Fueling: Gel at miles 6, 12, 18, 22. Water at every station.

Weather: [conditions from forecast]
Adjustments: [pace/fueling mods if heat, wind, rain]

Trust your training. Run your race. See you at the finish.
```

## Rules

- Every day has specific, actionable guidance — not vague encouragement
- Carb loading protocol is precise: timing, quantities, food types
- Logistics are explicit: bib, gear, travel, alarm
- Pacing strategy uses negative or even splits, never positive splits
- Race-day fueling is by mile marker, matched to the runner's tested protocol
- Nothing new on race day — food, gear, fueling, clothing all pre-tested
- Tone shifts from coaching to calm confidence as race day approaches
